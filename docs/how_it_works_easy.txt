HOW THE DIFFUSION WORLD MODEL WORKS (SLOW & EASY GUIDE)
=======================================================

Imagine you want to teach a robot to play Push-T (pushing a gray T-shaped block onto a
target spot on a 2D table using a blue circle agent).

In traditional Reinforcement Learning (RL), an agent learns a direct rule:
  "Look at the screen -> immediately guess an action."
That usually requires millions of trial-and-error interactions inside a simulator.

A World Model takes a different approach:
First, it learns a "mental physics simulator" from recorded gameplay videos. Then, at test
time, it imagines what will happen for different action plans inside its head and picks
the plan that reaches the goal.

Our system has three simple pieces:
  1. The Eyes    (Frozen SD-VAE, 83.7 M params, NOT trained by us)
  2. The Brain   (Diffusion Transformer, 23.3 M params, TRAINED by us)
  3. The Planner (MPC loop at test time)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PART 1: THE EYES — Frozen SD-VAE (83.7 M params, NOT trained)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A raw Push-T image is 224 x 224 pixels x 3 colors = 150,528 numbers per frame.
Predicting 150,528 pixel values step-by-step is slow and wastes effort on textures.

So we download a pretrained Stable Diffusion VAE (stabilityai/sd-vae-ft-mse):
  - Encoder (~34 M params): shrinks each 224x224 image down 8x into a tiny 4x28x28 grid
    (only 3,136 numbers — 48x smaller!), while keeping the exact positions of the blue
    circle and the T-block.
  - Decoder (~49 M params): turns a 4x28x28 grid back into a normal 224x224 image.
    We NEVER run the decoder during training or planning — only at the end when making
    GIFs and plots for your presentation!

Why is the VAE not trained?
  Because Stability AI already trained it on millions of images. It already knows how to
  see shapes, edges, and colors. We lock (freeze) its weights so training focuses 100%
  on Push-T physics.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PART 2: THE BRAIN — Diffusion Transformer (23.3 M params, TRAINED by us)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This 23.3 M parameter neural network (SpatioTemporalDiT) is the ONLY part we train.
Its job is to learn physics:
  "Given the last 3 shrunken frames and the push actions I took, what will the next
   shrunken frame look like?"

How is it trained? (Pure supervised learning — no simulator needed!)
  1. Take a short 4-frame clip from the recorded Push-T dataset:
       frame 0, frame 1, frame 2, frame 3   (each spaced 5 simulator steps apart)
  2. Shrink all 4 frames with the frozen Eyes (VAE).
  3. Keep frames 0, 1, 2 clean (this is the history/context).
  4. Take the real answer (frame 3) and mix it with random noise (like TV static),
     controlled by a noise slider tau between 0 (all noise) and 1 (clean frame).
  5. Give [frame 0, frame 1, frame 2, noisy frame 3] + [the push actions] to our
     23.3 M Brain and ask:
       "Which direction moves noisy frame 3 back toward the clean frame 3?"
  6. Measure MSE error between the Brain's predicted direction and the true direction,
     run loss.backward(), and update the 23.3 M Brain weights using AdamW.

That's it! Just standard deep learning: data -> forward pass -> MSE loss -> AdamW.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PART 3: THE PLANNER — Playing Push-T with MPC (Test Time)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Once the Brain is trained, we drop the agent into the real Push-T simulator and show it a
Goal picture (where the T-block should end up).

Every turn (every 10 raw simulator steps), the agent does:
  1. LOOK: Shrink the current table image and the Goal image using the frozen Eyes.
  2. IMAGINE: Propose ~64 candidate sequences of future push actions (looking 15 macro-steps
     ahead). For each candidate, start from blank/noise future frames and run the trained
     Brain for 4 quick denoising steps to "dream" the future table state.
  3. SCORE & IMPROVE: Compare the end of each dream against the shrunken Goal image (MSE).
     - GradientSolver backpropagates through the 4 dream steps to directly adjust the
       candidate push actions using AdamW so the dream lands closer to the goal.
     - (Or CEMSolver tries many random plans without gradients and keeps the best ones.)
  4. ACT & REPLAN: Execute only the first 2 macro-actions (10 raw steps) in the real
     Push-T game, take a fresh look at what actually happened, and replan from scratch.

Replanning frequently ("Model Predictive Control", MPC) guarantees that small imagination
errors never snowball over a 90-step episode.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ONE-SENTENCE SUMMARY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The frozen VAE (83.7 M) is the camera compressor, the trained Diffusion Transformer (23.3 M)
is the learned physics simulator, and MPC uses that simulator to test action plans in its
head before moving in the real world.
